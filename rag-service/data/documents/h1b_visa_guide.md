# H1B Visa Guide

## Overview
The H1B visa is a non-immigrant visa that allows US companies to employ foreign workers in specialty occupations that require theoretical or technical expertise. Technology companies, consulting firms, and other businesses often sponsor H1B visas for roles such as software engineers, data scientists, financial analysts, and other specialized positions.

## Eligibility Requirements

### Educational Requirements
- Bachelor's degree or higher in a specific specialty field
- Or equivalent work experience (typically 3 years of work experience = 1 year of college)
- The degree must be directly related to the job position

### Job Requirements
- Position must qualify as a "specialty occupation"
- Job requires theoretical and practical application of specialized knowledge
- Employer must demonstrate that the position normally requires a bachelor's degree

### Employer Requirements
- Must be a legitimate US employer
- Must file a Labor Condition Application (LCA) with the Department of Labor
- Must pay the prevailing wage or actual wage, whichever is higher
- Must be able to demonstrate the ability to pay the offered wage

## Application Process

### Step 1: Employer Files LCA
- Employer submits Labor Condition Application to Department of Labor
- LCA specifies job title, location, number of workers, and wage
- Processing time: typically 7 business days
- Must be certified before proceeding to H1B petition

### Step 2: H1B Cap Registration (if applicable)
- For cap-subject positions, employer must register during registration period (typically March)
- Registration fee: $10 per beneficiary
- USCIS conducts random lottery selection
- Must be selected in lottery to proceed with full petition

### Step 3: File H1B Petition (Form I-129)
- Employer files Form I-129 petition with USCIS
- Required documents include:
  - Certified LCA
  - Educational credentials evaluation
  - Job offer letter
  - Company documents (tax returns, organizational chart, etc.)
  - Evidence of specialty occupation
- Filing fee: $460 base fee + $500 fraud prevention fee + $1,500 (companies with 25+ employees) or $750 (companies with <25 employees) ACWIA fee
- Premium processing available for additional $2,500 (15-day processing)

### Step 4: Visa Stamping (if outside US)
- Schedule visa interview at US Embassy/Consulate
- Required documents:
  - Valid passport
  - Form DS-160
  - Form I-797 approval notice
  - Educational credentials
  - Employment letter
  - Recent photograph
- Interview typically takes 15-30 minutes

### Step 5: Enter US and Begin Work
- Must not begin work before the start date on Form I-797
- Present documents at port of entry
- I-94 will be stamped with H1B status

## Important Dates and Deadlines

- Registration Period: Typically early March (exact dates announced by USCIS)
- Lottery Results: Late March/Early April
- Earliest Start Date: October 1st of fiscal year
- H1B Cap: 65,000 regular cap + 20,000 master's cap

## H1B Extensions and Transfers

### Extensions
- Initial H1B valid for up to 3 years
- Can be extended for additional 3 years (total 6 years maximum)
- Extensions beyond 6 years possible if:
  - Green card process (PERM) filed at least 365 days before 6th year expiration
  - I-140 approved and green card not yet available due to visa bulletin backlog

### Transfers (Portability)
- Can transfer to new employer while in H1B status
- Can start working for new employer once new H1B petition is filed (receipt notice received)
- Does not count against H1B cap if transferred before current H1B expires

## Common Challenges and Solutions

### Challenge: Cap Lottery
- **Problem**: Only about 30% of registrations selected in recent years
- **Solutions**: 
  - Apply for cap-exempt positions (universities, research institutions)
  - Consider L-1 visa if working for same company in another country
  - Look for O-1 visa if have extraordinary ability
  - Consider Canadian/Mexican work permits (TN visa for eligible professions)

### Challenge: Maintaining Status
- **Problem**: Gap in employment can result in status violation
- **Solutions**:
  - Maintain valid status at all times
  - File for transfer before termination if changing employers
  - Consider change of status if employment ends (like B-2 or F-1)

### Challenge: Travel During Petition
- **Problem**: Leaving US during pending petition can abandon application
- **Solutions**:
  - Wait for approval before traveling if changing/extending status
  - If transferring, can travel with valid visa stamp from previous employer
  - Apply for advance parole if needed

## H1B vs Other Visa Types

### H1B vs L-1
- L-1 requires 1 year employment with foreign company
- L-1 not subject to annual cap
- L-1 allows for dual intent (unlike some other visas)

### H1B vs O-1
- O-1 for individuals with extraordinary ability
- O-1 has no cap or lottery
- O-1 requires higher level of achievement evidence

### H1B vs TN
- TN only for Canadian and Mexican citizens
- TN limited to specific NAFTA professions
- TN easier and faster to obtain but less flexible

## Family Members (H4 Visa)

- Spouse and unmarried children under 21 eligible for H4 status
- H4 holders can attend school/university
- H4 EAD (work authorization) available if H1B holder has approved I-140 or is in 6th year extension

## Costs Summary

- LCA Filing: Free
- H1B Registration: $10
- H1B Petition Filing Fee: $460
- Fraud Prevention Fee: $500
- ACWIA Training Fee: $750 or $1,500
- Premium Processing (optional): $2,500
- Visa Application Fee (DS-160): $190
- Attorney Fees: Typically $2,000-$5,000

**Total Estimated Cost: $4,000-$10,000+ (usually paid by employer)**

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I work for multiple employers on H1B?**
A: Yes, but each employer must file a separate H1B petition.

**Q: Can I do consulting work or freelancing?**
A: Only if employer files H1B petition for that specific work.

**Q: What happens if I lose my job?**
A: You have a 60-day grace period to find new employment or change status.

**Q: Can I start my own company?**
A: Yes, but complex. Company must sponsor your H1B and you cannot be sole owner/decision maker.

**Q: How early can I apply for extension?**
A: Up to 6 months before current H1B expires.
