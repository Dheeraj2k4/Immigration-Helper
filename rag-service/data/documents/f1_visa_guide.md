# F-1 Student Visa Guide

## Overview
The F-1 visa is a non-immigrant student visa that allows international students to study in the United States at an accredited college, university, seminary, conservatory, academic high school, elementary school, or other academic institution or in a language training program. F-1 students must maintain a full course of study and can work under certain circumstances.

## Eligibility Requirements

### Student Requirements
- Must be enrolled in an academic program or language training program
- Must be enrolled as a full-time student at the institution
- Must have proficiency in English or be enrolled in courses leading to English proficiency
- Must have sufficient funds to support themselves during the entire course of study
- Must maintain a residence abroad that they have no intention of abandoning

### School Requirements
- Must be approved by the Student and Exchange Visitor Program (SEVP)
- Must issue Form I-20 (Certificate of Eligibility for Nonimmigrant Student Status)
- Must maintain certification with SEVP

## Application Process

### Step 1: Apply and Get Accepted to SEVP-Certified School
- Apply to schools that are SEVP-certified
- Receive acceptance letter
- Pay SEVIS fee (I-901) online
- School will issue Form I-20

### Step 2: Pay SEVIS Fee
- Fee Amount: $350
- Pay online at fmjfee.com
- Print receipt for visa interview
- Must be paid at least 3 days before visa interview

### Step 3: Complete DS-160 Form
- Complete online nonimmigrant visa application
- Upload photograph meeting requirements
- Print confirmation page with barcode
- Keep DS-160 confirmation number

### Step 4: Schedule Visa Interview
- Schedule interview at US Embassy or Consulate in home country
- Wait times vary by location and season
- Summer months typically have longer wait times
- Schedule as early as possible

### Step 5: Prepare Documents
Required documents for interview:
- Valid passport (valid for at least 6 months beyond intended stay)
- Form I-20 from school
- SEVIS fee payment receipt
- DS-160 confirmation page
- Visa application fee receipt
- Passport-style photograph
- Academic transcripts and degrees
- Standardized test scores (TOEFL, SAT, GRE, etc.)
- Financial evidence showing ability to pay for education and living expenses
- Proof of ties to home country

### Step 6: Attend Visa Interview
- Arrive early at embassy/consulate
- Bring all required documents
- Answer questions about educational plans and intent to return home
- Provide biometric information (fingerprints)
- Interview typically lasts 3-5 minutes

### Step 7: Visa Decision
- Approval: Passport will be returned with visa stamp within 1-2 weeks
- Administrative Processing: May need additional review (can take weeks to months)
- Denial: Will receive written explanation of reason

## Entering the United States

### Entry Requirements
- Can enter up to 30 days before program start date on Form I-20
- Present at port of entry:
  - Valid passport with F-1 visa
  - Form I-20
  - SEVIS fee receipt
  - Financial documents
  - School acceptance letter
- CBP officer will stamp I-94 with "D/S" (Duration of Status)

### First Semester
- Must attend school and maintain full-time enrollment
- Check in with Designated School Official (DSO) within 30 days
- Begin taking courses
- Understand F-1 regulations and requirements

## Maintaining F-1 Status

### Full-Time Enrollment
- Undergraduate: at least 12 credit hours per semester
- Graduate: as defined by school (typically 9 credit hours)
- Can drop below full-time for medical reasons (with DSO approval)
- Can take reduced course load in final semester if fewer credits needed to graduate

### Academic Progress
- Must make normal progress toward degree
- Cannot fail too many classes
- Must maintain required GPA (varies by school)

### Registration in SEVIS
- School updates your status in SEVIS system
- Must report any changes to DSO:
  - Change of address (within 10 days)
  - Change of major
  - Change in financial support
  - Other material changes

### Passport Validity
- Must maintain valid passport at all times
- Renew passport before expiration

## Work Authorization for F-1 Students

### On-Campus Employment
- Can work up to 20 hours per week during school term
- Can work full-time during breaks and holidays
- No special permission needed
- Examples: library, cafeteria, administrative offices, research assistant

### Curricular Practical Training (CPT)
- Must have been enrolled for at least one academic year
- Must be part of established curriculum or related to major
- Can be full-time or part-time
- Requires authorization from DSO
- Must be listed on Form I-20
- Full-time CPT for 12 months or more makes you ineligible for OPT

### Optional Practical Training (OPT)
- Can work for up to 12 months in field related to major
- Pre-completion OPT: before graduation (part-time during school, full-time during breaks)
- Post-completion OPT: after graduation
- Apply on Form I-765 at least 90 days before desired start date
- Must apply before completing degree or within 60-day grace period
- STEM students may be eligible for 24-month extension (total 36 months OPT)

### STEM OPT Extension
- Available for students with STEM degrees
- Additional 24 months of work authorization
- Requires employer to be E-Verify participant
- Must submit Form I-983 (Training Plan)
- Must be related to STEM degree

## Travel While on F-1 Status

### Travel Signature
- Need travel signature from DSO on Form I-20
- Signature valid for 1 year for most students
- Valid for 6 months if using OPT

### Required Documents for Reentry
- Valid passport
- Valid F-1 visa (or can apply for new visa abroad)
- Form I-20 with valid travel signature
- Proof of enrollment (registration, transcript)
- Financial documents

### Visa Renewal
- F-1 visa stamp can expire while maintaining status
- Need new visa stamp to reenter US after travel
- Can apply for new visa at US Embassy/Consulate abroad
- Automatic visa revalidation for trips to Canada/Mexico of less than 30 days

## F-1 Grace Periods

### Initial Grace Period
- 30 days after program starts to enter US
- Must arrive before classes begin

### Program Completion Grace Period
- 60 days after completing program or OPT end date
- Must depart US, change status, or transfer to another program
- Cannot work during grace period (unless on approved OPT)

## Transferring Schools

### Process
- Accept admission to new SEVP-certified school
- Notify current school's DSO of intent to transfer
- Complete current semester
- Have new school issue new Form I-20
- Check in with new DSO within 15 days of transfer date
- No need to leave US or get new visa

### Timing
- Must complete transfer within 5 months of ending at previous school
- Must start at new school in next available semester

## Changing Education Level

### Examples
- Bachelor's to Master's
- Master's to PhD
- Community college to 4-year university

### Process
- New school issues new Form I-20
- Can continue on same F-1 visa
- Total OPT time: 12 months per education level (24 months STEM extension)

## Dependent F-2 Visa

### Eligibility
- Spouse and unmarried children under 21
- Must prove relationship (marriage certificate, birth certificate)
- Can attend school (elementary, middle, high school; college with change of status)
- Cannot work (no employment authorization)

### Application
- Apply for F-2 visa at same time or after F-1 principal
- Need F-1 student's I-20 and proof of financial support
- Can travel independently

## Common Violations to Avoid

### Status Violations
- Dropping below full-time enrollment without permission
- Working without authorization
- Overstaying grace period
- Failing to maintain valid passport
- Not reporting address changes

### Consequences
- Termination of SEVIS record
- Deportation
- Ban on reentry to US
- Difficulty obtaining future visas

## Costs Summary

- Visa application fee (MRV): $185
- SEVIS I-901 fee: $350
- School application fees: Varies ($50-$100 per school)
- Standardized tests: $200-$300
- Translation/evaluation of documents: $100-$500
- Travel to embassy: Varies
- Health insurance: $500-$3,000 per year
- OPT application (I-765): $410

## F-1 to Other Status

### F-1 to H1B
- Most common transition after graduation
- Employer must sponsor H1B petition
- H1B start date typically October 1
- Can use OPT or Cap-Gap extension to bridge between F-1 and H1B

### F-1 to Green Card
- Can pursue employment-based green card
- Can pursue family-based green card
- Maintain F-1 status while green card pending

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Can I take online classes while on F-1?**
A: Yes, but with restrictions. No more than one class or 3 credit hours per semester can be online for full-time requirement.

**Q: Can I take a semester off?**
A: Yes, but only for medical reasons or with DSO approval. Otherwise, you risk status violation.

**Q: What happens if I fail a class?**
A: One failing grade typically won't affect status, but repeated failures or low GPA can lead to status termination.

**Q: Can I change my major?**
A: Yes, report to DSO who will update SEVIS. May need new I-20 if significantly different major.

**Q: Can I travel during OPT?**
A: Yes, with valid visa, I-20 with travel signature, EAD card, and employment letter.

**Q: What if I lose my I-20?**
A: Contact DSO immediately to get replacement. Keep copies of all I-20s.
